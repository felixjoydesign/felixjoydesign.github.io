What to put on an invoice.

Every field below is already on the sheet above. This is what each one is for, and why leaving it out is what gets an invoice queried instead of paid.

  • Your business details

    Name, address, email, phone, and your tax registration number if you have one. This is who the money is owed to, so it belongs at the top.

  • Your customer's details

    The billing name and address of the person or company being charged. If you are invoicing a business, name the company, not just the person who hired you.

  • A unique invoice number

    Sequential, and never reused. It is how you and your customer refer to this one bill in every email that follows it.

  • Issue date and due date

    The day you sent it and the day you expect to be paid. "Due in 14 days" is a hope; a written date is a term.

  • A line for every item

    Description, quantity, unit price, line total. A single vague line for four weeks of work is the invoice that comes back with questions.

  • Subtotal, tax and total

    Show the tax as its own line, named the way your country names it (GST, VAT, sales tax) so the customer can check the arithmetic.

  • Discounts, shipping, deposits

    Anything that moves the final number gets its own line. A total that cannot be reconstructed from the lines above it looks like a mistake.

  • How to pay you, and the terms

    Bank transfer, UPI, PayPal or Wise, written out where it can be copied. Add late fees, warranty or a thank-you in the notes.

Sending it, and getting paid.

Making the invoice is the easy half. These are the habits that shorten the gap between sending it and the money arriving.

How to send an invoice

Email is the norm: attach the PDF and keep the covering note to two lines. Put the invoice number in the subject so it can be searched for later.

If you would rather not attach anything, paste the share link instead. It opens the finished invoice in the browser with a Download PDF button of its own.

When to send it

The day the work is finished, or the day the goods ship. An invoice that arrives a fortnight late has already taught the customer that the date on it is negotiable.

For ongoing work, bill on the same day every month and say so up front. For anything large, invoice a deposit before you start and the balance on delivery.

Getting paid on time

Put a real due date on it, not a phrase. Offer more than one way to pay. Quote the invoice number in every message about it.

Then follow up on a schedule rather than a feeling: a short note the day after it falls due, another a week later, and a phone call after that.

Invoice, receipt or quote: which one?

Four documents that look alike and do completely different jobs. All four can be made on the sheet above by changing the title and hiding what you do not need.

Invoice
A request for payment, sent after the work is done or the goods have shipped. It carries a number, a due date and the amount owed.
Receipt
Proof that payment has already been made. Same details, past tense. It confirms what was paid rather than asking for it.
Quote or estimate
A price offered before any work begins, usually with an expiry date. Nothing is owed yet, so it has no due date.
Proforma invoice
A provisional bill sent before delivery, often so a buyer can arrange payment or clear customs. It is not a demand for payment and is not filed as one.

Is this invoice generator really free?

Yes. Every part of it is free with no limit on how many invoices you make, and there is no watermark on the PDF. We build it because a lot of the people who need an invoice today need an online store builder tomorrow.

Do I need a Store.link account to use it?

No. There is nothing to sign up for and nothing to log into. Open the page, type, download. An account only matters if you decide to sell online with Store.link, and that is a separate decision.

Where is my invoice saved? Can you see it?

It is saved in your own browser, in local storage on your device, and it is never sent to us. We cannot see your invoices, your customers or your amounts. Clearing your browser data or using a different device means starting from a blank sheet.

Are these invoices valid for tax purposes?

The sheet includes the fields tax authorities generally ask for: your details, your customer, a unique number, dates, itemised lines and a separate tax line. What counts as a valid invoice still varies by country, so check the specifics where you file (a GSTIN, a VAT number or a fixed numbering rule, for example) and add it before you send.

Can I make a GST or VAT invoice?

Yes. Rename the tax line to GST, VAT, sales tax or anything else, set it as a percentage or a flat figure, and put your registration number in your business details. There is a TDS line too, for clients who deduct tax at source, and it can be relabelled as withholding tax.

Can I add my logo?

Yes. Drag an image onto the logo slot at the top of the sheet, or click it to pick a file. It is scaled down and stored inside the document itself, so it comes through into the PDF and, space permitting, into a share link as well.

Can I send an invoice without downloading a PDF?

Yes. Copy share link puts the entire invoice inside a URL, so you can paste it into an email or a WhatsApp message. Whoever opens it sees the finished document, read-only, with a Download PDF button of their own.

What invoice number should I start with?

Anything sequential that you will not lose track of. Plenty of businesses start at 0001, and some prefix a year or a client code, like 2026-014. The only rules that matter are that numbers never repeat and never go backwards.

Can I use it for a proforma or commercial invoice?

Yes. Change the document title to Proforma Invoice, Commercial Invoice, Quote or Receipt, then hide the sections that do not apply and add whatever the recipient needs in the notes.

Does it work on a phone?

It does. The sheet scales down and stays editable, and the PDF that comes out is the same A4 document. For a long invoice with many line items, a laptop is a kinder place to type it.
